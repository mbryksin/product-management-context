# OKRs — Overview

> Phase 1 — Strategy

OKR work translates company and product strategy into a cascading set of measurable commitments for each team and individual. It creates alignment across the organisation, surfaces conflicts between competing priorities, and provides a shared rhythm for progress tracking through regular check-ins.

## OKR Reference

### Structure

An OKR consists of one **Objective** and two to five **Key Results**.

- **Objective** — a qualitative, inspiring statement of what you want to achieve. Written in plain language. No numbers.
- **Key Result** — a measurable outcome that proves the objective was achieved. Specific, time-bound, and binary (you hit it or you don't).

### Writing rules

| Element | Good | Avoid |
|---------|------|-------|
| Objective | "Make onboarding so good that new users see value in their first session" | "Improve onboarding" |
| Key Result | "Increase Day-1 activation rate from 32% to 55% by end of Q[X]" | "Work on activation" |

### Cadence

- Set OKRs at the start of each quarter (company → team → individual cascade).
- Weekly check-ins: update confidence score (1–10) and flag blockers.
- Mid-quarter review: adjust if context has fundamentally changed.
- End-of-quarter scoring: 0.0–1.0 per key result; 0.6–0.7 is a healthy stretch.

### OKR Template

**Objective:** [Fill in: what do you want to achieve this quarter?]

| # | Key Result | Owner | Start | Target | Current | Confidence |
|---|-----------|-------|-------|--------|---------|------------|
| 1 | [Fill in: measurable outcome] | [Name] | [Baseline] | [Goal] | [Current value] | [1–10] |
| 2 | [Fill in: measurable outcome] | [Name] | [Baseline] | [Goal] | [Current value] | [1–10] |
| 3 | [Fill in: measurable outcome] | [Name] | [Baseline] | [Goal] | [Current value] | [1–10] |

### Common pitfalls

- Output OKRs ("launch feature X") rather than outcome OKRs ("increase retention by X%")
- Too many OKRs — limit to 3 objectives with 3–4 KRs each
- No baseline — always record the starting value so progress is measurable
- Set-and-forget — OKRs require weekly attention to be useful
