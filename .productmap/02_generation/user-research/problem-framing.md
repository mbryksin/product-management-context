# Problem Framing

**Competency:** Discovery

## Summary

Clearly define the problem or opportunity before jumping to solutions, so the team works on the right thing. Good problem framing prevents building the wrong thing with great execution.

## Key Concepts

### Problem Statements

A problem statement captures who is affected, what they are trying to do, and what gets in their way — without specifying a solution.

**Template:**

> [User type] trying to [goal] experience [problem/friction] because [root cause], which causes [impact].

**Example:**

> A junior product manager trying to prioritise their backlog experiences analysis paralysis because they lack a clear scoring framework, which causes delayed sprint planning and stakeholder frustration.

### How Might We (HMW) Questions

HMW questions reframe a problem as an open invitation for solutions. They are used in workshops and ideation sessions to generate options.

**Template:**

> How might we [help user type] [achieve goal / overcome barrier] [within constraint]?

**Example:**

> How might we help PMs confidently prioritise competing features in under 30 minutes?

### Avoiding Solution Bias

Common failure modes:
- Framing the problem as a missing feature ("users can't do X") rather than an unmet need ("users struggle to achieve Y")
- Jumping to "we should build Z" before understanding root cause
- Anchoring to the first solution suggested in a meeting

Use the "5 Whys" technique to get from symptom to root cause before writing your problem statement.
