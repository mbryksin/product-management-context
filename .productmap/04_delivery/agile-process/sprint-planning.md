# Sprint Planning

**Competency:** Delivery

## Summary

Plan time-boxed work (sprints or iterations) so the team commits to achievable scope and aligns on outcomes. Effective sprint planning converts backlog items into a shared commitment with a clear goal and realistic capacity.

## Key Concepts

### Sprint Goals and Capacity

- A sprint goal is a single sentence describing the outcome the team is targeting, not just a list of tasks.
- Capacity planning: sum available days minus planned leave, then apply a velocity factor (typically 70–80% of raw capacity to account for meetings and unplanned work).
- Commit to scope that fits capacity — avoid overpromising.

### Backlog Refinement and Commitment

- Backlog should be refined before sprint planning: stories are written, estimated, and understood by the team.
- During planning, the team confirms understanding of each item and collectively commits — not just the PM.
- If a story is unclear or too large, break it down before committing.

### Ceremonies and Follow-Through

- **Sprint planning:** 1–2 hours for a 2-week sprint. Walk through candidate items, confirm estimates, set the sprint goal.
- **Daily standup:** 15 minutes. What did I do yesterday, what am I doing today, any blockers?
- **Sprint review:** Demo completed work to stakeholders. Capture feedback.
- **Retrospective:** What went well, what to improve, one action for next sprint.

---

## Sprint Planning Checklist

### Before the Sprint

- [ ] Backlog is groomed: top items are written, sized, and understood
- [ ] Team capacity is calculated (available days × velocity factor)
- [ ] Sprint goal candidate is drafted by PM before the session
- [ ] Dependencies and blockers are identified and flagged

### During the Sprint Planning Session

- [ ] Review and confirm the sprint goal with the team
- [ ] Walk through candidate stories — confirm clarity and estimates
- [ ] Identify any items that need further breakdown before starting
- [ ] Confirm commitment: team explicitly agrees to the sprint scope
- [ ] Assign initial owners if useful (or let team self-assign during sprint)

### After the Sprint

- [ ] Sprint board is set up with all committed items
- [ ] Sprint goal and items are visible to stakeholders
- [ ] Any carry-over items from previous sprint are accounted for

---

## Sprint Goal Template

> This sprint we will **[achieve outcome]** so that **[business or user value]**. We'll know we succeeded when **[measurable condition]**.

**Example:**

> This sprint we will ship the onboarding redesign to 20% of new users so that we can measure its impact on Day-1 activation. We'll know we succeeded when the variant is live and tracking events are confirmed working.
